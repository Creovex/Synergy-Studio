# Action and comedy sound recipes

Each recipe says what to layer. "Synth" means a procedural sound (noise, sine sweeps); "GM" means a SoundFont
instrument or drum note (gm-map.md). Put the transient exactly on the cue; let anticipation lead in before it.

| Moment | Recipe | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Big impact** (landing, slam, title hit) | GM Orchestra Hit 55 + timpani 47 (low note) + crash 49 + synth sine drop 80→40 Hz | a 1–2 s riser or reverse cymbal 119 ending on the cue; a camera shake on the same frame |
| **Bonk** (something hits a head) | synth pitch-falling pop + GM wood block 76 + a short boing (synth vibrato sine) | follow with 0.3 s silence for the reaction |
| **Door slam** | GM low floor tom 41 + kick 36 + synth noise burst through a low-pass | add a rattle (fast 32nd snare roll at low velocity) |
| **Whoosh** (fast pass, camera whip) | synth band-passed noise swelling and falling (0.3–0.6 s) | pan it with the motion; pitch it up for small objects |
| **Riser** (tension into a reveal) | synth noise or saw sweep up + GM reverse cymbal 119 + a timpani roll (fast 32nds, velocity rising) | ends exactly on the reveal cue, then silence for 1–2 frames |
| **Reveal / logo sting** | GM brass 61 chord + glockenspiel 9 sparkle + crash 49 | the brand's motif in 3–5 notes if there is one |
| **Magic / sparkle** | GM harp 46 glissando up + celesta 8 or glockenspiel 9 + synth shimmer | glissando = 8–12 notes in 0.3–0.5 s |
| **Slip / fall** | GM slide whistle (flute 73 or ocarina 79 with a downward pitch bend) + a final low tom 41 thud | the bend takes the whole fall; set the channel's bend range to 12–24 semitones first (the default ±2 is too small) |
| **Jump / boing** | synth vibrato sine sweep up, or flute 73 bent up | timing: starts at take-off, peaks at the top |
| **Tiptoe / sneaking** | GM pizzicato 45 staccato notes, one per step, chromatic up | low velocity; pairs with a bassoon 70 |
| **Running** | GM wood blocks 76/77 alternating fast, or xylophone 13 runs | tempo of the steps |
| **Failure / "nope"** | GM trombone 57: four notes falling (wah, wah, wah, waaah) with a pitch bend down on the last | or a muted trumpet 59 for a smaller fail |
| **Success / win** | GM brass 61 or strings 48: a rising arpeggio to a held major chord + crash | or a marimba 12 run up for small wins |
| **Tech / UI** (taps, screens, notifications) | synth blips (short sines 1–3 kHz), clicks and soft whooshes | GM vibraphone 11 for a friendlier notification |
| **Phone ringing** | GM Telephone Ring 124 | or synth: two sines 1300 + 1650 Hz gated at 22 Hz |
| **Crowd / applause** | GM Applause 126 at low level under the music | |
| **Birds / outdoors** | GM Bird Tweet 123, a few notes, panned | |
| **Heartbeat / suspense** | GM kick 36 at low velocity, twice per beat (lub-dub), at 60–70 BPM | low-pass everything else |
| **Thunder / storm** | synth low noise rumble with a long decay + GM timpani roll | flash on the picture at the crack |

## Rules of thumb
- Fewer, better sounds. Sound only the actions that carry the story; the rest share one bed.
- Match the size: small things are high and short; big things are low and long.
- Leave space after a hit. The audience laughs or feels it in the silence.
- When music and an effect fight, duck the music 3–6 dB under the effect for its length.
