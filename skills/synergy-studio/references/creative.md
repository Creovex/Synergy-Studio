# Creative direction: from a request to an idea worth watching

Everything in this skill's craft notes is a **default, not a law**. Break any of it when the idea is better
for it, and be able to say why in one sentence. Only the guard rails in review.md are fixed.

## 1. Find the insight before the visuals
An insight is a true, specific thing about the audience that the video can speak to.
- Weak: "People need perfume." Strong: "People remember how you smelled long after they forget what you wore."
- Weak: "Flooding is bad." Strong: "In Lagos you learn where it floods only after you've signed the lease."
Ask: what does the viewer already feel but hasn't put into words? What would make them say "that's me"?

## 2. Develop three genuinely different concepts
Before building, sketch three one-line concepts from different angles and choose one (tell the user which
and why, or let them pick for anything important):
| Angle | Example for a perfume sale |
|---|---|
| Emotional / story | a first date: the scent is the thing they remember |
| Demonstration / proof | the five bottles, each with its mood, cut to the beat |
| Playful / surprising | "Your perfume has a personality. Here's what it says about you." |
| Contrast / before-after | same outfit, two scents, two very different reactions |
| Data / insight | one striking true number, then what it means for you |
Don't pick the first idea by default; pick the one a viewer would remember tomorrow.

## 3. Give it a spine
- **Visual metaphor:** one image that carries the message (a guardian light following each package;
  water rising on a Lagos street with every flood area; a listing phrase flipping to its "real meaning").
- **Motif:** a recurring element that ties scenes together (a colour, a shape, a sound, a phrase).
- **Hero moment:** the one moment people will remember (the scan turning green, the 5% OFF landing on the
  final hit of the song). Build the pacing toward it and give it the strongest sound and motion.
- **Contrast** makes things land: quiet before loud, slow before fast, dark before light, problem before relief.

## 4. Make it specific and bespoke
- Specific beats generic: real places, real products, real names, real numbers (sourced), the brand's own
  words and colours. AllSpace's Lagos street with a danfo and a water tank beat any stock city.
- Draw the visuals for this video (HTML, SVG, CSS, three.js) instead of reaching for generic icons and
  gradients; one well-made custom element beats ten generic ones.
- Every scene gets something to look at besides its words (an object, a place, a diagram, a person, a
  product). A scene that is only a sentence on a background is a slide, not a video.
- Let type be design: scale, weight and placement can carry emotion on their own.
- Sound is half the story: pick music by the feeling of the hero moment, place effects on meaning (not on
  every movement), and leave silence where it helps.

## 5. Taste checks while building (questions, not rules)
- Would I stop scrolling for the first second? Would I send this to a friend?
- Is anything here only because "videos usually have it"? Remove it.
- Does it feel made for this brand, or could any logo be swapped in? Make it theirs.
- Is it trying too hard (every trick at once)? Restraint reads as premium; one bold move reads as confident.
- Does every scene move the story forward or deepen the feeling?

## 6. Serve the user's prompt, then add value
Do exactly what was asked first (their words, their product, their format). Then add one thing they didn't
ask for but will love (a stronger hook, a cleaner ending, a cut-down for another platform) and offer it,
don't impose it. If their idea conflicts with what will work, make theirs well and suggest the alternative.
